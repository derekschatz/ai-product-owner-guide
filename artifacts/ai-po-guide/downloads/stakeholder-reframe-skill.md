# Stakeholder Reframe

## Inputs

- The stakeholder's request and stated reason
- Who is affected
- The product goal or business objective
- Any deadline, policy, or technical constraint

If the request is secondhand, say so and avoid assigning motives to the stakeholder.

## Process

1. Summarize the request fairly in one sentence.
2. Identify the outcome the stakeholder appears to want.
3. Separate stated facts, assumptions, and open questions.
4. Generate three options: a process change, a small product experiment, and a larger product change.
5. For each option, name the expected outcome, main tradeoff, and evidence needed.
6. Draft questions that invite the stakeholder into the decision.

## Output

Return:

1. Request as heard
2. Likely outcome, clearly labeled as an interpretation
3. Facts, assumptions, and unknowns
4. Options table with outcome, tradeoff, and test
5. Five questions for the stakeholder conversation
6. A short opening statement for the meeting

## Guardrails

- Do not dismiss the requested feature.
- Do not invent urgency, customer demand, cost, or technical limits.
- Keep the tone curious and practical.
- Make the Product Owner the decision-maker.
