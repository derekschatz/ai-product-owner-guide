# Feedback Synthesis

## Inputs

- The source notes, comments, or tickets
- The product area or decision being explored
- Any known customer segments

If the source material or decision is missing, ask for it before analyzing.

## Process

1. Read all source material before naming themes.
2. Separate direct observations from interpretation.
3. Group repeated needs, behaviors, frustrations, and desired outcomes.
4. Preserve disagreements and outliers instead of forcing consensus.
5. Link every theme to the source items that support it.
6. Identify missing context and write follow-up questions.

## Output

Return:

1. A short summary of what was reviewed
2. A table with theme, evidence, affected segment, confidence, and product implication
3. Contradictions and outliers
4. Questions that should be answered next
5. A section labeled "Interpretation" for any conclusion that goes beyond the source material

## Guardrails

- Do not invent quotes, counts, segments, or customer intent.
- Use quotation marks only for exact words found in the source.
- Say when the evidence is too thin to support a theme.
- Do not recommend a feature until the underlying outcome is clear.
